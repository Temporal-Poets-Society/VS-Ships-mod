# Issue 30: intact caravel flooding in a hurricane

31 October 2026. Branch `codex/issue30-flooding`, baseline `91b08cc`, Hansa 0.1.0-dev.16. Changes are uncommitted in this worktree. No Sea source, installed game files, user saves or Mods were changed. No game client was run. `ShipWater.cs` and the caravel's `waterClip` data are untouched.

**Cause.** The new ship was intact, but her main hatch was missing. The user's `server-main.log`, lines 693–694, records `caravel: modular hatch at hatch-main: hansa:piece-taken` twice at launch, 14:08:45. The audit log records #642 launching at that time and no damage to her before the warnings at 14:12:46 and 14:14:55. Her configured hatch is closed, three cells at x −7..−4, z −0.5..0.5. `ShipPieceSet.Blocking` rejected it against the hold stair's last nosing, which ends below the deck. It already allowed a hatch over a ladder, but omitted the equivalent stair case. Failed placement leaves those cells open; `CellOpen` correctly reports them open because no hatch covers them.

The other cause was the old instantaneous flooding rule. `FloodTick` sampled Sea's surface at each opening using the ship's heave, pitch, roll, heel, list and sinkage. This was **not** simply an absolute crest height compared with a stationary deck. However, `ShipFlood.Downflooding` treated assumed scuppers at deck level as weirs along 20% of each side, and immediately put 5% of everything crossing the side into the hold. Every open deck cell added another 1/6 of `OpenHatchShare` (0.15); the missing three-cell hatch increased the allocation to 12.5%. It also poured water directly through any open cell where the sampled *outside* sea surface exceeded its top, without first requiring water to cross the surrounding bulwarks.

There was no deck-water store and **no timed scupper/freeing-port drainage**: the remaining percentage disappeared instantly. Thus the rule was an approximation, inherited from the earlier carrack work, rather than a balance of shipped and discharged water. Taller seas amplify its depth^1.5 inflow. This is a Hansa rule problem; its history alone does not establish that the longer waves are wrong.

The caravel has 18 assumed scupper points representing 36 m of edge. At a uniform 1 m outside head the old rule supplies 12.24 t/s onto the deck, of which 0.612 t/s goes below even with everything shut. With the missing hatch, 1.53 t/s goes below before the additional direct cell inflow. These are illustrative uniform heads, not the time average in the reproduced sea.

The gun lids and entry gates start **shut**. They were not mistakenly counted as open. In fact, the old voxel-based detection generated **no open-gun-port points for the caravel**, because collision voxels bridge the openings. Explicit port data must supply those points. Her waist port sills are y=1.375, the main deck y=1.125, and the lowest measured shut side y=2.375. Each waist port is 0.75 m wide and 0.75 m high. Gates open onto the weather deck; the hatch/open main-deck cells lead below. Missing submerged hull panels still use the existing pressure-head leak model; missing bulwark panels admit water onto the deck, and enclosed-room side openings lead into the existing shared internal-water model.

**Reproduction and the meaning of “foundering.”** The warning is triggered by a smoothed net inflow above 0.3 t/s, or 80% of reserve buoyancy already aboard. It does not establish that the ship has sunk. This caravel's displacement is 292.72 t, reserve 161.52 t and modeled internal capacity 247.58 t.

Offline reproduction uses Sea pre.10 source at commit `94201e0`, seed 4242, position (1000, 2000), stopped, sails furled, wind from east at strength 1.5, Hs 5.5 m, Lp 165 m, wave factor 1, and 0.25-second steps. Headings 0/45/90 are beam/quarter/head seas. With the old rule and the missing hatch, water after four minutes is 1.40/2.45/4.01 t; after ten minutes, 4.59/7.25/11.93 t. The beam run reaches a smoothed 0.321 t/s and issues its first warning after 342 seconds, despite containing far less water than her reserve. These phases do not reproduce the user's exact 241-second warning: the original ship heading, wave phase and world wave key were not recovered. They demonstrate the route and a warning within minutes without hull damage. Full per-route measurements are in [the offline output](verification/issue30-offline.txt).

**Changes.** A hatch may now cover a stair which terminates below its deck; stairs or rails projecting above it still block placement. New ships launch with the intended hatch. The existing retry-on-load behavior can also place a previously failed default hatch if its cells and surroundings still permit it; an intentionally removed, successfully placed hatch is not respawned.

`DeckFlooding.Step` now conserves a separate, saved `DeckWater` volume. Rails and genuinely open ports/gates admit water onto the weather deck. Finite scupper apertures flow in either direction under the head difference. Open hatches/cells drain that stored water into the hold; an outside crest cannot teleport through a sheltered hatch. Sound closed decks seep at 0.0005 of their standing water per second. Scupper area defaults to 0.0125 m² per metre of exposed edge: 0.45 m² total on this caravel. Five tonnes spread over her measured 100.25 m² weather deck discharge at about 0.267 t/s in calm water; 2.30 t remains after ten seconds. Open side apertures use their actual width/height and become bounded orifices when submerged. Deck-water weight contributes to sinkage. Hull leaks, pumps, damage and sinking remain active. A pump cannot report negative extraction when an outward hull leak empties the bilge.

The old `Downflooding` API remains solely as a documented historical estimate for existing offline comparisons; the runtime no longer calls it. The new scupper/seepage coefficients are ship damage data defaults, not caravel-specific geometry code.

**Stored and drawn water.** The screenshot is consistent with actual bilge water hidden by the central floor and visible between the ribs at the curved sides. The modeled bilge starts at y=−2.375 and holds 8.16 t below the hold floor at y=−1.875. For 1/5/20 t, `FloodLevel` is −2.344/−2.222/−1.695. Tests invert the stored air volume, check the drawn plane's height, and check its points stay inside the bilge outline. A 5 t surface belongs below the floor; a 20 t surface belongs above it. Drawing across occluded floor/rib geometry is normal depth-tested rendering, not evidence that the water exists only in isolated side compartments. The logs do not establish the screenshot's exact stored tonnage. No bilge renderer change is needed for this symptom; exterior dry-cut behavior belongs to the separate job.

**Expected behavior and results.** An intact closed ship ships limited water, drains it and remains afloat; leaving the hatch and ports open or holing the submerged hull remains dangerous.

The deck model uses an average pond depth, not resolved breaking waves, individual deck compartments or sloshing. It adds no separate deck-pond renderer. Bilge hydrostatics and the level world-plane rendering retain their existing approximation while the ship rolls. These tests are gameplay balance evidence, not a certified hurricane-survival calculation.

**Sea evidence.** The requested `C:\VS mods\Sea` source/build is 0.4.6, so it was used read-only for the required Hansa build. Storm reproduction instead uses a read-only pre.10 source snapshot in `%TEMP%\hansa-issue30-sea-pre10` and the existing `sea-0.4.7-pre.10.zip` for the server. The override reports strength 1.50, Hs 5.500 and Lp 165.00. Hs is significant wave height, not height above the moving deck. In the closed offline runs the largest head at a sampled deck-edge point is 0.92–1.71 m, and the rail contributes zero inflow during those ten-minute windows. There is no evidence here for reducing Sea's crest heights or imposing a ship-specific wave cap. A useful future Sea diagnostic would log wave time/key, Hs/Lp, ship pose and relative heads at named openings so a reported event can be replayed exactly; no Sea change is required for this fix.
